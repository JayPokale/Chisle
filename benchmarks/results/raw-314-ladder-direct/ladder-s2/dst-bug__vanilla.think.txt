The issue:
- A nightly job is scheduled with `setInterval(run, 24 * 60 * 60 * 1000)` 
- It drifts by one hour after every daylight saving change
- This happens predictably with DST changes

The root cause:
When you use `setInterval` with a fixed millisecond interval like 24 hours in milliseconds, it will execute exactly 24 hours apart in wall-clock time (elapsed milliseconds). However, a "day" or "nightly" expectation is usually based on calendar/clock time.

When daylight saving time changes:
- In spring (spring forward): clocks go forward 1 hour. So midnight to midnight is only 23 hours of elapsed time. If you're using `setInterval(run, 24 * 60 * 60 * 1000)`, the next execution will be 24 elapsed hours later, which is 1 hour into the next day.
- In fall (fall back): clocks go back 1 hour. So midnight to midnight is 25 hours of elapsed time. If you're using `setInterval(run, 24 * 60 * 60 * 1000)`, the next execution will be 24 elapsed hours later, which is 1 hour before the next midnight.

So the job drifts by an hour after each DST change because `setInterval` measures elapsed time (wall-clock seconds), not calendar days.

The fix is to NOT use `setInterval` for time-of-day scheduling. Instead, you should:
1. Calculate when the next scheduled time should occur (e.g., next midnight or next occurrence of a specific time)
