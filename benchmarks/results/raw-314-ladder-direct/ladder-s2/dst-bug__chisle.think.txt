This is a classic daylight saving time (DST) bug. Let me think through the root cause.

The issue: a nightly job drifts by an hour after every DST change.

Setup: `setInterval(run, 24 * 60 * 60 * 1000)` — that's 86,400,000 milliseconds, which equals 24 hours in wall-clock time.

**The Problem:**
- `setInterval` uses JavaScript timers, which are based on **elapsed real time** (milliseconds), not wall-clock time.
- 24 * 60 * 60 * 1000 = 86,400,000 ms = exactly 24 real hours.
- When DST ends (fall back), the wall clock moves backward 1 hour. The interval still fires after 24 real hours have elapsed, but the wall-clock time has "drifted" backward by 1 hour from when the job should run.
- Similarly, when DST begins (spring forward), the wall clock jumps ahead 1 hour. The interval fires after 24 real hours, but it's now 1 hour later on the wall clock than intended.

**Why this happens:**
The scheduler doesn't track wall-clock time or calendar time—it just waits 86,400,000 ms and fires. It has no concept of "run at midnight every night." After a DST shift, the job's firing time has drifted relative to the intended wall-clock time (midnight).
