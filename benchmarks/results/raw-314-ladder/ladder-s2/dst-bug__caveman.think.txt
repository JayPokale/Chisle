The user is describing a nightly job that drifts by an hour after every daylight saving time (DST) change, and it's scheduled with `setInterval(run, 24 * 60 * 60 * 1000)`.

The root cause here is that `24 * 60 * 60 * 1000` is 86,400,000 milliseconds, which is exactly 24 hours. However, when daylight saving time changes occur, a day is NOT 24 hours:
- When DST starts (spring forward): clocks move forward 1 hour, so the day is 23 hours long
- When DST ends (fall back): clocks move back 1 hour, so the day is 25 hours long

Using a fixed 24-hour interval means:
- After spring forward (day is 23 hours): the job runs 1 hour earlier than expected
- After fall back (day is 25 hours): the job runs 1 hour later than expected

The drift accumulates with each DST change.

The fix is to NOT use `setInterval` for scheduling daily jobs. Instead, use a scheduler that understands calendar dates and can reschedule based on when the next calendar day occurs, independent of wall-clock time.
